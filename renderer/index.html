VHS Commercial Cutter — Guide

VHS Commercial Cutter finds the commercials in a digitized VHS tape so you can save them as clips. It can also do the reverse and keep just the show.

Quick start

  1. Open a file — click Open file… (top-right) or drag a video onto the player. A small preview builds in the background so scrubbing is fast — watch the badge at the top-left of the video. Detection scans that preview because it's far quicker and finds the same breaks; export always uses your full-quality original.
  2. Detect commercials — click Detect commercials. It scans for fade-to-black + audio silence (the marks between show and ads) and splits the tape into clips, with the ads defaulting to save.
  3. Review — clips appear on the timeline and in the list, colored green = save / red = skip. Click one to play it, and fix any mistakes (see Editing).
  4. Choose what to export — in Export, pick Commercials or The show, then One merged file or Separate clips.
  5. Export — choose an output folder if you like, then Export. Files save next to your source by default.

The two jobs

  • Commercials — the main event: exports the clips you saved. Separate clips gives you one file per ad; one merged file gives you the whole ad break in one.
  • The show — the reverse: exports what you skipped, commercials removed. Usually one merged file.

Editing clips

Detection gets you most of the way; fine-tune the rest:

  • Click a clip to select and preview it. Click its save/skip tag (or press K) to flip it.
  • Drag the yellow boundary handles on the timeline to move where a clip starts/ends.
  • S split the selected clip at the playhead · M / Shift+M merge with the previous/next clip.
  • I / O snap the selected clip's in / out point to the playhead.

Detect settings

  • Black sensitivity — how dark a frame must be to count as a break. Raise it if breaks are missed.
  • Silence threshold — how quiet the audio must drop at a break.
  • Min commercial gap — ignores tiny gaps (noise).
  • Max commercial length — clips shorter than this are guessed to be commercials.

Tune without scanning the whole tape: scrub to a spot you know has a commercial break, then use Test here (under the Detect button) to scan just a short sample. Candidate breaks show as cyan marks on the timeline. Adjust the sliders and re-test until it catches the break, then run full detection. If breaks are still missed, raise Black sensitivity / Silence threshold; if it over-cuts, lower them and raise Min commercial gap. Stations that never fade to black can't be detected — add those cuts by hand with S.

Restore (optional)

  • Repair torn frames — some capture devices lose sync a few times a second and write a frame split across two positions with a band of corruption between. On screen it looks like a stutter, so it's easy to mistake for a frame-rate problem. This rebuilds those frames from the ones either side. Every tape is checked when you open it, and this switches on by itself if tearing is found — you don't need to go looking for it. It's a fault in the capture hardware rather than the tape, so it usually affects everything captured on the same setup. Leave it off otherwise: it makes detection noticeably slower.
  • Enhance — denoise + re-sharpen by tape speed (SP / LP / EP). Use heavier presets for older, noisier tapes.
  • Correct color — brightness, contrast, saturation, gamma, plus Red/Green/Blue balance to remove color casts.
  • Audio drift — shift the audio to fix lip-sync (positive = audio later).

Export settings

  • What to export — the saved commercials, or the skipped show.
  • Frame — keep the source shape, or reframe to 4:3 (1440×1080). Reframing centre-crops, which takes the black pillars off a 4:3-in-16:9 capture and leaves the picture whole.
  • Resolution · Frame rate · Quality — output size and encoding (Archive → Low).
  • Output filename — a custom name for the files. One merged file vs Separate clips.

Keyboard

Space play/pause · ←→ step frame (Shift = 1 sec) · Home/End · S split · M merge · I/O set in-out · K save/skip
